# Seasons: free data integration plan

Research and public endpoint probes: 2026-11-01. No paid subscriptions or accounts created. No personal trip data transmitted; probes used country codes, a public region code, and example airport codes.

## Follow-up: non-European visitor demand (2026-11-02)

| Candidate | Free access and coverage | Limitation | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tourism Statistics API (`tourismapi.com`) | Free-key `GET /v1/seasons?limit=1000` returns country-level monthly seasonality indices (`100` means average). The current free tier has 1,000 calls/month, one year of history delayed by about one year, and free access to the seasons endpoint. Its current catalog says 215 countries/territories are held, but only 123 publish monthly observations; other countries may be annual-only. A free key requires Google sign-in; no card or paid plan is required. | The free seasons endpoint is a national seasonality index, not a city crowd count. Detailed arrivals/months endpoint is on the paid Developer tier. Coverage and freshness vary by country; use each row’s `years`/`through` metadata. Requires optional `TOURISM_API_KEY`; terms permit display but prohibit bulk redistribution. | Already implemented as a cached fallback outside Eurostat’s regional coverage; retain Eurostat where available. Cache the global seasons response for 90 days and each year’s bulk holiday response for 365 days. Persistent per-key caching plus a one-request/day cap for each endpoint limits normal use to about two calls in a month and worst-case use to at most 62 calls in any calendar month, well below 1,000. Do not require or use a paid tier. |
| UN Tourism seasonality dashboard powered by Telefónica | Global destination dashboard advertises monthly arrivals/receipts, monthly annual share, and a 10-year seasonality indicator. | The public page embeds Power BI; no supported public API, downloadable dataset license, or integration terms were found. | Research lead only; do not scrape or integrate until a documented free API/license is available. |
| National open-data portals (for example India and Singapore) | Some provide free monthly official arrival series and API/download access. | Separate schema, units, arrival definition, and terms for each country; no single global interface. | Add only country adapters for destinations with a trusted public API if future coverage justifies the maintenance. |

No verified free source currently provides globally comparable, multi-year, city-level visitor counts. Keep Eurostat for regional Europe and use TourismAPI's free country-seasonality summary only as an explicitly country-level fallback. The fallback is not a city crowd count and its lookback must be taken from each row's metadata. The free tier's current documentation reports monthly coverage for 123 of 215 held countries, so it still cannot fill every destination. Country portals such as India's Ministry of Tourism publish official monthly arrivals, but each requires a separate adapter and does not solve global coverage; only add one when it materially covers our trips and a stable machine-readable endpoint and reuse terms are confirmed.

## Adapter smoke check

On 2026-11-01, the source paths were called once using the disposable `client/e2e/.tmp/e2e.db`: Nager returned 16 Japanese national holidays; the Paris pilot returned 12 monthly groups; NOAA returned covered Atlantic data for all 12 months; GISCO mapped Athens to EL30; Eurostat produced 12 monthly observations. A second process reused persisted responses and made zero provider requests. The separate GISCO country-boundary endpoint resolved Tokyo to JP. The Paris pilot was later removed from the product because its coverage was limited to Paris. No Open-Meteo request was made because that provider was already under cooldown.

## Existing code to reuse

- `server/src/services/vacayService.ts` already proxies Nager v3 holidays in multiple places. Extract a shared holiday service, migrate its provider response into the existing Vacay contract, and use it for Seasons too. Do not create a second holiday fetching path.
- `weatherService.ts` already batches coordinate lookups, stores historical monthly climate in SQLite and shares provider cooldowns. Extend the climate fields and bump the versioned cache key when changing aggregation.
- `mapsService.reverseGeocode` returns country codes, but places do not persist a country code. Resolve geography once per unique destination, cache it, and retain source/version. Avoid reverse geocoding every place on each page load.
- `TripSeasonsView.tsx` and `tripSeasonsModel.ts` already group nearby route places and weight by assigned days. Preserve the trips-by-month matrix and route breakdown.

## Implementation sequence

1. **Implemented:** shared holiday cache used by Vacay and Seasons. Fetch country/year with Nager v4, store successful responses and provider cooldown/budget state in SQLite, preserve country subdivision metadata and source, and expose national holidays by explicit year. Italy falls back to keyless OpenHolidays; nine other uncovered ISO countries use one optional, cached TourismAPI bulk calendar request per year. Unsupported/unavailable calendars remain unknown. Public holidays flag possible closures or domestic travel, not measured crowd levels.
2. **Implemented:** compact factor selector in the existing trips-by-month matrix for weather, regional visitor demand, year-specific public holidays and historical cyclone tracks. A selected month opens detail with coverage/provenance. Factors are independent and missing data is not treated as zero.
3. Free crowd baseline. **Implemented:** map destination coordinates to GISCO NUTS-2 2024 polygons; request only unseen regions from Eurostat; select annual frequency, total residents, I551-I553 accommodation, nights, and the latest four complete years (currently 2022–2025); calculate month-length-adjusted monthly nights relative to the annual daily average; preserve provisional flags; cache boundaries and observations in SQLite. The UI describes this as an accommodation-demand proxy, not attraction footfall, and leaves unsupported routes unknown. Route areas are deduplicated by NUTS2 and weighted by itinerary days. Outside Europe, the optional TourismAPI national monthly index is used when available; it is country-level, not city footfall, and countries without monthly rows remain unknown. GISCO country aliases `EL`/`UK` are normalized to `GR`/`GB` before this fallback. Holiday country lookup uses a separate cached GISCO 2024 WGS84 country boundary layer worldwide.
4. **Deferred:** structured events are not integrated. The tested Paris feed had insufficient geographic coverage. No replacement should be added until a no-cost source offers broadly useful international coverage and acceptable reuse terms.
5. **Implemented regional pilot:** versioned NOAA NHC HURDAT2 Atlantic and NE/NC Pacific files are fetched only when the factor is first selected, parsed into SQLite, and reused for ten years. The factor deduplicates storm IDs and reports unique nearby tracks per 100 years (1980–2025, 150 nautical mile radius). These are historical context flags, not forecasts or personal risk estimates. A coarse longitude split in the overlapping Americas bounds keeps each destination to one basin file/request; coastal boundary locations can be misclassified. Other basins (including western Pacific) remain unknown. Replace the split with official basin geometry before expanding coverage.
6. **Implemented Natural hazards factor:** USGS FDSN supplies global M6+ earthquakes from 1980–2025 with one cached query; NOAA NCEI HazEL is the primary paginated tsunami/volcano source. Smithsonian GVP supplies a cached dated eruption fallback when NOAA is unavailable; USGS tsunami-flagged earthquakes are the labeled partial tsunami fallback. Provider budgets and persisted backoff prevent repeated calls. Events are summarized within 150 nautical miles; monthly activity is only shown after five or more local events. These catalogs are not complete hazard inventories. Cyclone history remains Atlantic and eastern/northeastern Pacific only. This is context, not a forecast or personal safety score.
7. Flight-price feasibility gate. Verify free Travelpayouts credentials/terms and test representative routes before implementation. Require departure airport, trip length, currency and market. Display recent observed cached fares with observation/expiry timestamps and sample coverage. Its cache is based on other users' searches and can omit routes or dates; missing results are not high prices. It cannot support a complete historical cheapest-month claim. Keep optional and off until credentials are configured.

## Retrieval and storage

Use a small server-side Seasons aggregation service within the existing Nest application, with provider-specific adapters and additive SQLite caches. Resolve and warm data when destinations change. Dashboard requests read cached factors and enqueue missing work; they must not fan out to all providers. Store source keys, time periods, geographic resolution, retrieval timestamps and anomalies. Fixed historical snapshots remain versioned; holidays and event dates refresh. Never silently substitute stale event dates or prices. Respect attribution, feed licenses and source-specific retention rules before storing or redistributing payloads.

## Acceptance checks for implementation

- Existing Vacay holiday consumers retain their response contract; unsupported countries show unavailable rather than zero holidays.
- Reopening Seasons and restarting the backend reuse persisted successful data; repeated failures respect provider backoff.
- Eurostat parsing selects the intended dimension cells, validates complete months, accounts for month length, and preserves official flags.
- Route weighting does not multiply one city by its number of attractions; an important missing route leg shows partial coverage.
- Do not present city-specific event feeds as a route-wide seasonal factor; require broad geographic coverage before integration.
- Cyclone fixture parsing, basin coverage and durable snapshot cache are tested; unsupported regions remain unknown.
- Desktop and mobile screenshot workflow verifies factor selection, month details, partial coverage and no-data states.
